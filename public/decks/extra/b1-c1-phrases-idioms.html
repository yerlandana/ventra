B1 to C1Speaking

Giving your opinion

Skip “I think” every time.

Tip

Use one new opinion phrase per answer, then give a reason.

B1C1
01

I think…

It seems to me that…

It seems to me that online lessons suit busy people.

02

In my opinion…

As far as I’m concerned,…

As far as I’m concerned, homework should be optional.

03

I believe…

I’m inclined to think that…

I’m inclined to think that practice matters most.

04

I like the idea of…

I’m all in favour of…

I’m all in favour of learning two languages at once.

05

I’m sure that…

I’m convinced that…

I’m convinced that daily reading improves speaking.

Agree and disagree

Be polite and still be clear.

Tip

After you disagree, always add because or for example.

B1C1
01

I agree.

I couldn’t agree more.

Exams are stressful. I couldn’t agree more.

02

You’re right.

That’s a fair point.

That’s a fair point. I hadn’t thought of it that way.

03

I disagree.

I see your point, but…

I see your point, but it doesn’t work for everyone.

04

I don’t think so.

I’m not entirely convinced.

I’m not entirely convinced that money brings happiness.

05

Maybe yes, maybe no.

I’m in two minds about it.

I’m in two minds about working from home.

Buying time

Replace “um” with a phrase.

Tip

A short pause sounds more confident than um.

B1C1
01

Um… er…

Let me think for a moment.

Let me think for a moment. I’d say it was last spring.

02

I don’t know.

That’s a tricky one.

That’s a tricky one. I’ve never really thought about it.

03

Well…

To be honest,…

To be honest, I’m not a big fan of fast food.

04

I mean…

What I’m trying to say is…

What I’m trying to say is that time matters most.

05

Oh, yes…

Now that you mention it,…

Now that you mention it, I do spend hours online.

Reasons and results

Link ideas in a more precise way.

Tip

Use one linking phrase in each answer, not three.

B1C1
01

because of

owing to

Owing to the traffic, I arrived late.

02

because of (good)

thanks to

Thanks to my teacher, I passed with 7.5.

03

so

consequently,…

Prices rose. Consequently, many shops closed.

04

this makes…

This has led to…

This has led to a rise in remote work.

05

The bad thing is…

The downside is that…

The downside is that it costs a fortune.

Contrast and balance

Show both sides of an idea.

Tip

Balanced answers fit Part 3 questions about advantages and disadvantages.

B1C1
01

but

Having said that,…

It’s expensive. Having said that, it’s worth every penny.

02

On the other hand,…

On the flip side,…

On the flip side, big cities are noisy and crowded.

03

and also…

What’s more,…

What’s more, you can study from anywhere.

04

but (in a sentence)

whereas

I love cities, whereas my sister prefers villages.

05

however,…

Nevertheless,…

Nevertheless, I would still recommend it.

Hedging and speculating

Sound careful, not absolute.

Tip

Avoid always and never in Part 3. Hedging sounds more thoughtful.

B1C1
01

maybe

It could well be that…

It could well be that prices will fall next year.

02

probably

Chances are,…

Chances are, you’ll need a visa.

03

usually

tend to

Young people tend to spend more time online.

04

most people

By and large,…

By and large, people in my city are friendly.

05

a little

To some extent,…

To some extent, social media brings people closer.

Stronger words

Replace very + adjective with one word.

Tip

Say absolutely exhausted. Never say very exhausted.

B1C1
01

very important

crucial

Sleep is crucial for memory.

02

very big

enormous

There has been an enormous change in how we work.

03

very good

outstanding

The service was outstanding.

04

very bad

dreadful

The weather was dreadful all week.

05

very tired

exhausted

After the exam I was absolutely exhausted.

Idioms for work

Use one or two per answer, no more.

Tip

Add a real detail after the idiom so it sounds natural.

01

hit the nail on the head

say exactly the right thing

You hit the nail on the head: the real problem is time.

02

get the ball rolling

start something

Let’s get the ball rolling with a short warm-up.

03

go the extra mile

make more effort than needed

She always goes the extra mile for her students.

04

a piece of cake

very easy

The listening test was a piece of cake.

05

on the right track

doing something correctly

Keep practising, you’re on the right track.

06

back to square one

start again from the beginning

My file was lost, so I’m back to square one.

Keep your chin up, things will get better.

06

Idioms for problems

Talk about difficult situations and choices.

Tip

Idioms are for speaking. Avoid them in Writing Task 2.

01

a blessing in disguise

a bad thing that turns out good

Losing that job was a blessing in disguise.

02

between a rock and a hard place

two bad choices

I’m between a rock and a hard place: pay more or move.

03

bite off more than you can chew

take on too much

I took three courses and bit off more than I could chew.

04

the tip of the iceberg

a small part of a big problem

Those complaints are just the tip of the iceberg.

05

cut corners

do something badly to save time or money

Don’t cut corners on your essay plan.

06

think outside the box

think in a creative way

To solve it, we had to think outside the box.

Grammar
trick 1

Start with “what”. The key idea comes last.

Use it for

Likes, dislikes and the best part of a story.

01

B1I love the food.

C1What I love most is the food.

02

B1The price surprised me.

C1What surprised me was the price.

03

B1My city is safe, and I like that.

C1The thing I like most about my city is how safe it is.

04

B1My teacher made me confident.

C1It was my teacher who made me confident.

13
B1 to C1Speaking

Grammar
trick 2

Add “which” to extend any short answer.

Use it for

Part 1 answers. Add a comma and which after your first idea.

01

B1I work late. I am often tired.

C1I work late, which is why I am often tired.

02

B1I live near the sea. It is relaxing.

C1I live near the sea, which helps me relax after work.

03

B1I passed the exam. I did not expect that.

C1I passed the exam, which I honestly didn’t expect.

04

B1Traffic is bad. I cycle.

C1Traffic is awful, which is why I cycle to work.

Grammar
trick 3

Talk about past and imaginary situations.

Use it for

Part 3 questions about change, regret and the past.

01

B1I didn’t study, so I’m not a doctor.

C1If I’d studied harder, I’d be a doctor now.

02

B1My teacher helped me. I passed.

C1If it weren’t for my teacher, I wouldn’t have passed.

03

B1I regret moving to the city.

C1I wish I hadn’t moved to the city.

04

B1I didn’t save money, so I can’t travel.

C1If I’d saved more, I could be travelling now.

Your speaking plan

Five habits for the next month.

01

Learn 3 phrases a day

Write them in your phone, one topic at a time.

02

Use each one in your own sentence

About your family, work or city.

03

One idiom per answer

More than two sounds unnatural.

04

Add “which” to one answer a day

Practise until it is automatic.

05

Record and listen back

Two minutes a day. Check pace and pronunciation.

Target

About 20 new phrases a week.
